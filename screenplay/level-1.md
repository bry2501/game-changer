## Evidence 0: The household log (the prior)

*On the side table in the study. The player searches it; the evidence screen opens.*

**Evidence card: the household log, tonight (6 – 10 pm)**

| Name | Signed in | Signed out | Inside, 6–10 pm |
|---|---|---|---|
| Graves | — | not in tonight (evening off) | — |
| Clara | 7:00 pm | 10:00 pm (still in) | 3 h |
| Mr. Voss | 4:00 pm (before the window) | 8:00 pm | 2 h |
| Pip | — | out 6:00 pm (went home) | — |

> **GHOST** *(thought)*: My own rule, and I remember the day I made it: everyone signs the household log when they come and go, the vicar included. A house should know who is in it. It shows who was inside tonight between 6 pm and 10 pm, and for how long. Graves had asked for the evening off to visit his sister; I remember saying yes. Pip went home at six, as he always does. That leaves two people who were in this house with my tea: Clara and Mr. Voss. Everything I weigh from here on is a contest between those two. If I have overlooked a third, none of it will be worth much.
>
> **GHOST** *(thought)*: Before I weigh a single clue, I need somewhere to stand. I do not yet know when anyone could have reached my tea, so I will count the hours each of them spent under this roof: the longer someone was here, the more chances they had. That starting belief is called the PRIOR. It is a judgment, not a fact. And I will not rule anyone out completely: a belief set at zero can never be moved by any clue, however good.

**Clara** · *Clara was inside for 3 hours.* Everyone together: 5 hours (shown as half-hour blocks, Clara’s lit).
The lit blocks are Clara’s share of all the time anyone spent inside. More time inside means more chances to reach my tea, so a bigger starting share of suspicion.

**Mr. Voss** · *Mr. Voss was inside for 2 hours.* Everyone together: 5 hours (shown as half-hour blocks, Mr. Voss’s lit).
The lit blocks are Mr. Voss’s share of all the time anyone spent inside. More time inside means more chances to reach my tea, so a bigger starting share of suspicion.

**Where my suspicion starts**: Each person’s share of the time inside becomes their starting share of suspicion. *(a single bar, names only)*

**What the starting point means**: Before any clue, suspicion starts where the opportunity was: whoever spent longest in the house starts highest. It is only a starting guess, not a verdict. Every clue from here will shift it.
Every clue I find from now on will shift these shares. How far each one shifts them is the whole art.

> **Designer’s note (not shown in game):** prior = hours inside ÷ total: Clara 3/5 = 60%, Mr. Voss 2/5 = 40%. Clara starts ahead purely on opportunity.
>
> - The prior is a *subjective* starting point (degree of belief), and hours-inside is a transparent stand-in for it. Any coherent starting point is legitimate; what matters is that the evidence is allowed to move it.
> - The log is also what closes the hypothesis set. The stretch step in every update is only valid because “Clara” and “Mr. Voss” are mutually exclusive and cover every possibility. The ghost says so out loud; keep that assumption visible in later levels, and give any third suspect a share.
> - Neither share is ever 0% or 100%, and no rating maps to 0 or 1, so no belief can become immune to later evidence.
> - The hours-inside rule counts time before the tea existed. That is acceptable only because the ghost does not yet know when the tea was tampered with. A stricter alternative would count only the half hour the cup stood on the desk.

> **GHOST** *(thought)*: Clara was here the longest, so she starts as the likeliest. That is opportunity, not guilt. The man nearest the door is not therefore the thief.
> **GHOST** *(thought)*: Every clue from now on should shift these shares. By how much is the whole question.
> **GHOST** *(thought)*: Clara’s room is upstairs. I know my niece. When she is angry, Clara writes. She always has: pages of it, scorching, and then she never sends them. If she wrote to me after our quarrel, the draft will be in her writing desk.

### Page: Update (the player steps through)

- **Before**: Before the clue. Each bar is one person’s share of my suspicion. Together they fill the whole line.
- **Step 1 of 3: shrink**: Each share shrinks by how well the clue fits that person’s story. A good fit keeps most of it; a poor fit keeps little. The faded part is what the clue took away.

## Appendix: the intended reasoning chain (designer’s notes)

**How ratings become numbers.** A rating is the likelihood P(clue | this person did it): 1 → 0.1, 2 → 0.3, 3 → 0.5, 4 → 0.7, 5 → 0.9. It never reaches 0 or 1, so no story can be ruled out, or proved, by one clue. Only the *ratio* of the two ratings moves the board, which is why a clue rated the same for both suspects changes nothing.

The board after each clue, if the player’s ratings match the intended ratings (a model answer, not an answer key):

| After | Ratings (Clara / Mr. Voss) | Likelihood ratio | Clara | Mr. Voss |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Start (household log) | — | — | 60% | 40% |
| 1. An Angry Letter | 4 / 3 | 1.4 : 1 for Clara (weak) | 68% | 32% |
| 2. A Muddy Boot Print | 1 / 3 | 5 : 1 for Voss (strong) | 30% | 70% |
| 3. The Partnership Ledger | 2 / 5 | 3 : 1 for Voss (moderate) | 12% | 88% |
| 4. The Telephone Log | 1 / 3 | 5 : 1 for Voss (strong) | 3% | 97% |
| 5. A Cufflink Engraved "V" | 3 / 4 | 1.4 : 1 for Voss (weak) | 2% | 98% |

The old chain gave the letter (“vivid but weak”), the ledger (“genuinely diagnostic”) and the cufflink (“modest”) the same strength, 2.33 : 1, so the lessons contradicted the numbers. Strength now matches the lesson: weak, weak, moderate, strong, strong. The ledger is deliberately only 3 : 1 because the ghost himself allows an honest supplier.

**Checks run** (by the generator, every time). Every posterior above is computed from the ratings. The final board is the same for any order of the five clues. Starting Clara at 10%, 50%, 90% and 99% (same ratings) ends at 0.1%, 1.3%, 10.7% and 56.9%: priors wash out only when everyone shares the same likelihoods.

**Assumptions the game makes (keep them visible in later levels).**

1. One culprit, and the two suspects cover every possibility, so the stretch step is valid.
2. Each clue’s likelihood depends only on who did it, not on other clues found (conditional independence given the culprit). Watch pairs that share a story.
3. Background facts (Voss parks by the garden gate; Graves and Pip were out) are established before a clue uses them.
4. Verbal labels map to fixed numbers for every player. People read “likely” differently, so the labels and numbers must stay locked together.
